How HaulBook prices a job

1. Volume first. Junk is priced by space in the truck: load fractions (¼ / ½ / ¾ / full) × your truck's cubic-yard capacity, or enter cubic yards directly. The rate card price is volume × your $/yard rate, never below your minimum charge, plus heavy-item upcharges and any stairs/access fee.

2. True cost. Labor (crew × rate × hours) + disposal (estimated weight from material type × your dump $/ton) + travel (miles × cost/mile), plus your overhead %.

3. Three prices. Break-even = true cost + overhead. Recommended = the higher of your rate-card price and the price that hits your target margin — so a heavy load that costs more to dump than the rate card expects gets caught. Premium = recommended + your markup, for rush/weekend/nasty jobs.

4. The profit floor. Below-floor prices (under your minimum margin or under your minimum charge) trigger a red warning and block saving until you raise the price or deliberately override. Overrides are your call — the floor just makes sure it's never an accident.

Make the numbers yours (10 minutes)

Defaults are national planning numbers: $45/cubic yard (band $30–65), $65/ton dump fee, 250/400/900 lb per yard for light/mixed/heavy. Weigh a few real loads at your transfer station, compare against the estimates, and adjust the densities and rates in Settings until HaulBook's numbers match your scale tickets. A calculator is only as good as its inputs — this one makes yours easy to fix.

Honest limits

Weight is estimated from volume and material type — your scale ticket is the truth, and completed jobs record the real weight. HaulBook doesn't dispatch, invoice, or take payments; it's the pricing and job-profit book. Hazardous materials, eviction cleanouts with legal holds, and anything your local rules restrict are your call as the operator — the app prices junk, it doesn't clear you to haul it.
